So What Makes Us Special?

A Colony For You

Now, we know about the horrible times Jamestown has suffered. We owe it to our brave colonists who've endured the horrible "Straving Time" that paved the way for our lovely community to thrive. See, it was these ancestors who discovered the life fuel the colony runs on - tobacco. Now, whether you're a smoker or not, the crop has maintained an infuencial role in the growth of our community, and continues to boost the colonial economy.

Through struggle, our community has grown strong. One part of that growth is something that sets us miles apart from the other colonies - our very own governing system. The House of Burgesses, first established in 1619, is proudly the FIRST EVER legislative English assembly within the Americas. Jamestown, along with the other settlements in the Virginia Colony, have the priviledge of having elected officials, as well as parliment representatives, discuss and vote on contemporary affairs, ensuring that important issues are always addressed and taken care of.

Our Life in Jamestown

Climate and Environment

Because of our location, Jamestown has a nice, sunny climate with cold winters. The nice sun mixed with bodies of water like the James River make Jamestown a haven for growing crop, like tobacco!

Our Culture

Jamestown prides itself on our culture of legislative representation. While other colonies recieve broad laws from the King, we make it our prioirty at Jamestown to address our colonists issues. The House of Burgesses - for our people, by our people!

Demographics

Despite previous hostile relations with the Powhatan Confederacy, the intermarriage between John Rolfe and Pocahontas has created a strong alliance between Powhatans and Englishmen. Even so, we pride ourselves with our defensive milita, protecting us from threats.

Why Settlers Are Drawn Here

Jamestown is naturally an economically based colony, placing making profits rather than social relations with fellow Natives. Furthermore, the soil of Jamestown combined with nearby water sources make it excellent for farming tobacco. Also, the multitude of indentured servants willing to work would've provided another source for the workforce, as well as slaves imported from the Caribbean. For this reason, Jamestown would probably mostly attract Spanish colonists, who would most likely utilize either slavery or an encomienda system to take advantage of farming tobacco to generate profit.
